Before we talk about right now, we need to establish that what's happening right now has happened before — and that it was later proven to be deliberate.

The Senate Intelligence Committee's bipartisan report concluded the intelligence assessments used to justify the war were unsupported by or contradicted by the underlying intelligence. Colin Powell, who delivered the case to the UN Security Council, later called that presentation "a blot" on his record. There were no weapons of mass destruction.

This is not disputed. It is in the official record. It happened. It was documented. And then the people who ordered it retired comfortably, while veterans waited years for VA appointments and developed cancers from burn pit exposure that the DoD denied for over a decade.

Iraq is not an argument. Iraq is a precedent. The pattern exists. It has been proven to exist. Keep that in mind for what comes next.

## Part Two: What Is Happening Right Now

Operation Epic Fury launched February 28, 2026. Nine months ago.

The debate over the intelligence justification should sound familiar. Senate Intelligence Committee ranking Democrat Mark Warner and former counterterrorism official Joe Kent both argued publicly that the intelligence did not substantiate an imminent nuclear or military threat. Analysis of U.S. intelligence assessments indicated that Iran lacked active weaponization capability and did not possess intercontinental ballistic missiles capable of reaching the United States before 2035 — and that earlier strikes had already severely damaged or halted enrichment efforts.

No ICBM capability reaching the US before 2035. Enrichment already degraded by prior strikes. What was imminent?

Analysts warned the strikes risked regional escalation, disruption of the Strait of Hormuz — through which approximately 20% of the world's oil supply passes — and reconstitution of the very programs the strikes were meant to destroy.

The Senate Intelligence Committee is raising the same objections they raised about Iraq. The intelligence disputes are structurally identical. The "imminent threat" framing is the same framing. The contradictions are being documented in real time, right now, by the same oversight bodies that later confirmed Iraq's justification was manufactured.

You are not being asked to protect America from an imminent threat. You are being asked to repeat a documented pattern.

## Part Three: The Legal Status of the Orders You Are Following

This is not a moral argument. This is a legal one.

The War Powers Resolution requires that when the President introduces armed forces into hostilities without a declaration of war, he must notify Congress within 48 hours and withdraw forces within 60 days unless Congress authorizes the conflict. The 60-day clock on Operation Epic Fury expired in late April 2026.

**Congress has not declared war.**

A conflict running without Congressional authorization after the War Powers clock expires is illegal. The orders issued in furtherance of that conflict are unlawful orders.

This is not a gray area. There are no constitutional exceptions that extend presidential war authority beyond this window without Congressional authorization. The law is unambiguous. The clock ran out.

As Captain Lee Adama put it in a different context, with the same principle at stake:

> *"I swore an oath. To defend the articles. The articles say there is an election in seven months. Now, if you are telling me we are throwing out the law, then I am not a captain, you are not a commander, and you are not the president. And I don't owe either of you a damned explanation for anything."*

The oath is to the Constitution. Not to the person holding the office. When they throw out the law, the chain of command does not persist. You are not obligated to a commander who has placed themselves outside the legal framework that created the rank. That is the structure of the oath you took.

This is the structure of the law you swore to uphold.

### What the UCMJ Actually Says

Under Articles 90, 91, and 92 of the Uniform Code of Military Justice, it is a criminal offense to follow an unlawful order. The Manual for Courts-Martial defines an order as unlawful if it is *"contrary to the Constitution, the laws of the United States, or lawful superior orders."*

An order that violates the Constitution is legally void. You have no UCMJ obligation to follow it. More than that: **you are legally required to disobey it.**

The "I was just following orders" defense was rejected at Nuremberg. It is explicitly rejected in U.S. military case law — including *United States v. Calley*. Executing an order that violates the Constitution or federal law makes you personally criminally liable under the UCMJ.

You took an oath to the Constitution, not to the Commander in Chief. Those are not the same thing. The Constitution grants Congress — not the President — the power to declare war. That power has not been exercised.

**The Nuremberg Principles are not a political talking point.** They are binding international law that the United States helped write, following a war in which American soldiers died to establish the principle that "just following orders" does not absolve personal criminal liability for participation in unlawful acts.

The apparatus is asking you to assume all legal risk on its behalf. The same apparatus that has a documented history of manufacturing the justifications for the conflicts it sends you into is now asking you to accept personal criminal liability for following orders it may not have had the authority to issue.

That is the mercenary's bargain in its clearest form.
