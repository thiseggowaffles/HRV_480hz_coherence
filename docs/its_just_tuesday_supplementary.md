## FOIA — Freedom of Information Act Requests

**What it sounds like:** Investigative journalism. Adversarial transparency methodology.

**What it actually is:** Public records retrieval. The digital equivalent of asking to see the receipts.

The Freedom of Information Act legally mandates that federal agencies make records available to the public upon request. The practical reality is that navigating this requires knowing where to look and how to ask. Here are the basic search operators that actually work:

- `site:*.gov [topic] FOIA` — federal agencies generally
- `site:*.mil [topic] FOIA` — military branches
- `site:cia.gov FOIA [topic]` — CIA's FOIA reading room specifically

**The CIA library friction pattern:** Expect the search link to fail on the first attempt. Return and try again. It almost always works on the second try. Whether this is a technical artifact or deliberate friction design is an open question. If deliberate, it would constitute an unlawful barrier to legally mandated public access. We are not claiming it is deliberate. We are noting the observable pattern and its legal implications. We encourage you to test this yourself and form your own conclusions.

The goal of unnecessary friction, if intentional, is straightforward: most people don't come back. Persistence is the entire skill. The documents are there. The law says they have to be. You just have to ask twice.

## Security Through Obscurity and Its Defeat

The AATIP and AAWSAP documents — the Pentagon's Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program and Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program, obtained via FOIA — are publicly available. They are also written in advanced physics notation: Alcubierre metric equations, quantum vacuum plasma thrusters, traversable wormhole mathematics, exotic matter requirements for faster-than-light propulsion.

This is security through obscurity. FOIA compliance is technically achieved by releasing documents that the overwhelming majority of requesters cannot parse. The information is public. It is also, practically speaking, inaccessible to anyone without a graduate-level physics background.
