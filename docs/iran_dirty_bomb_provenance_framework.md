## Historical Pattern: When Intelligence Provenance Fails

### Case Study One: Iraq WMD, 2002-2003

The intelligence justifying the 2003 invasion of Iraq included:

- **Yellow cake uranium from Niger:** Documents later confirmed as crude forgeries. The IAEA identified inconsistencies within days of receiving them. These documents formed a central pillar of the public case for war.
- **Mobile bioweapons laboratories:** Based primarily on a single source, codenamed Curveball, later identified as a known fabricator who had been flagged as unreliable by his German handlers. DIA analysts had serious doubts that were not reflected in finished intelligence products.
- **Aluminum tubes for centrifuges:** Subject of significant internal dispute between agencies. The State Department's Bureau of Intelligence and Research dissented from the assessment that the tubes were intended for uranium enrichment.
- **Al-Qaeda connections:** Asserted with confidence in public statements. The 9/11 Commission found no credible evidence of a collaborative operational relationship.

**Pattern identified:** Intelligence with known reliability problems, originating from interested third parties, was laundered through the finished intelligence process and presented to the public and Congress with false confidence. The result was a war that produced hundreds of thousands of casualties.

**Provenance note:** Significant portions of the case for the Iraq invasion were constructed from intelligence provided by or through parties with direct material interest in an American invasion — specifically Iraqi exile groups (the Iraqi National Congress, led by Ahmed Chalabi) and Israeli intelligence, both of whom had strategic reasons to prefer American military engagement with the Baathist government.

### Case Study Two: Operation Northwoods, 1962 (Declassified)

Operation Northwoods was a proposal by the US Department of Defense Joint Chiefs of Staff, submitted to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara in 1962. It proposed:

- Staging Cuban attacks on American military assets
- Blowing up a US ship and blaming Cuba
- Fabricating evidence of Cuban aggression
- Creating a pretext for military intervention in Cuba

The document is real. It is declassified. It is available in the National Security Archive. It was not implemented — it was rejected by the Kennedy administration. Its significance is not what it caused but what it demonstrates: that the deliberate fabrication of a casus belli for war was considered, formally proposed, and staffed through the official military planning process by senior American military leadership.

**The pattern Northwoods demonstrates is not theoretical.** It is documented. The question it raises about any subsequent intelligence claim is not paranoid — it is epistemologically required.

**Observation:** A dirty bomb attack on Washington DC is inconsistent with Iran's stated doctrine, inconsistent with Iranian strategic interests, and would constitute the single most irrational military action available to Iranian leadership.
